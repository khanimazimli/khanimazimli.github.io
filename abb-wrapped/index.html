01

Executive overview

The goal: give every app user a short, shareable story of their own year, and make that repeatable every December.

ABB Wrapped (in the app: “ABB Hekayəniz”, your ABB story) is the bank's annual year-in-review. The 2025 edition shipped in December 2025 as a set of designed story screens. For 2026, I reshaped it into a system: story templates that take each customer's values at runtime, separate versions for mass-market and premium customers, and a tracked production pipeline from concept to backend hand-off.

The 2026 edition has not launched. The story set is built and has passed my pre-handover QA; product-owner approval, backend integration and the bank's own testing are still ahead.

02

Business problem

How do you turn individual customer data into one coherent story when every customer had a different year?

A year-in-review is a strong engagement moment, but a bank's version carries risks a music app's does not. A wrong amount, an awkward message about spending, or an empty story for a customer who barely used the app damages trust rather than building it.

The 2025 edition answered that with careful production: designed screens, reviewed one by one. Doing it again that way would mean repeating the whole production effort every year. The question for 2026 was structural:

  • Which parts of a customer's year are worth telling, and in what order?
  • How can one template serve fifty countries and every spending category without breaking?
  • Should premium customers see a different story, and what changes if they do?
  • How do we hand a large story set to backend and product teams in a state they can trust?

04

Strategic approach

Treat the story as a content system, not a set of screens.

The 2025 edition showed what kinds of year exist. It used 13 story families with 113 content entries in total: top spending category (19 variants), top country abroad (51), cashback package (10), product “firsts” (10), loyalty, lottery and brand stories (12), separate paths for churned and new customers (8), and three shared screens.

2026 kept that architecture and changed how it is built:

  • Templates instead of screens. One master country template generates fifty country stories. Each template declares the values it expects, such as {{country}}, {{amount}}, {{category}} and {{cashback_package}}, and must hold category names of very different lengths without breaking.
  • A fallback for every family. A country outside the designed set gets a generic version with its name filled in. No customer gets a broken or empty screen.
  • Separate paths for edge segments. A customer who left the bank, or joined late, does not have a year to summarise, so they get their own sequence instead.
  • Audience versions. Mass-market and premium customers receive different versions of selected stories.

    Implementation and collaboration

    1. Concept and alignmentConcept work started in July 2026; a set of idea screens laid out the direction before production began.
    2. Template buildStories were built as HTML templates with declared runtime values, part of them produced with AI assistance and reviewed by me.
    3. Tracked pipelineEach story moves through in progress, ready for review, product-owner review, ready for backend, integration, QA and done.
    4. Production boardA single view of every file by topic, audience and version, with logo checks and hand-off status, so nothing reaches the backend team unreviewed.
    5. Pre-handover QAAn automated check of every file before hand-off: layout at phone sizes, motion, dynamic values, console errors, assets, logo placement, and performance on a simulated low-end Android device.

    08

    Lessons and transferable capabilities

    Design the edge cases first.Personalization fails at the margins: rare countries, churned customers, thin data. Fallbacks and separate paths are what make it safe to scale.
    Content architecture set the cost and the risk.Choosing which kinds of year to tell, and how many variants each needs, decided the production load, the timeline and where things could go wrong.
    Check before you hand over.Testing every file on a slow phone before hand-off moves problems to the cheapest point to fix them.
    Templates should make the next edition cheaper.Runtime templates and a tracked pipeline are meant to turn a yearly production effort into a maintained system. 2026 will be the first test.
