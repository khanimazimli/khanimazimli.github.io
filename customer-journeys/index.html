Customer Journeys & Hyper-Personalization: in the bank's hyper-personalization squad, I decide who a campaign is for, when it should reach them, and when the bank should stay silent.

Problem
Many teams want the same customers' attention. Sending everything is noise, not personalization.
My role
Lead Customer Experience Specialist. I own my campaigns on the business side.
Why it was complex
Five stages with different owners, Risk and Finance approval, three languages and bank-wide contact rules.
Delivered
Live campaigns I own, with further campaigns in approval or development.
01 The business problem

More messages do not mean better personalization.

Every campaign can be approved on its own and still add up to noise for the customer. The decision that matters is per customer, not per campaign.

Broadcast

Every approved campaign goes to everyone

Each customer receives several messages, whether or not they fit.

Decided

Each customer gets the one that fits, or none

Some receive one relevant message. Others are deliberately held back.

03 Analytical thinking

Setting a spend threshold, worked through.

A reward should change behaviour, not pay customers for what they already do. Move the threshold and watch the trade-off.

  1. 01
    Business question

    Which spend threshold makes a task-and-reward campaign add spending, at a reward cost Finance will approve?

  2. 02
    Hypothesis

    The right threshold sits a little above what most target customers spend today: close enough to reach, high enough to need a stretch.

  3. 03
    Synthetic evidence

    Monthly card spend for 10,000 invented customers. The real analysis uses each customer's recent spend.

  4. 04
    Trade-offs

    Customers who already qualify cost money without changing behaviour. Customers far below it are unlikely to try.

  5. 05
    Decision rule

    Choose the threshold where the within-reach group is large and most rewards go to customers who must stretch, within the cost Risk and Finance approve.

  6. 06
    Validation

    A control group receives no message, so the effect is the difference between groups: added spend per customer, reward cost per added AZN, and opt-outs as a guardrail.

04 My responsibility

What I decide, and what other teams own.

For the campaigns I own, I set the business logic. I do not build the scoring models or configure the marketing platform.
