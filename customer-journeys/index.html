Case 02 · Retail banking · Mobile appCampaigns live

How do we turn customer information into better business decisions?

In the bank's hyper-personalization squad I decide who a campaign is for, what triggers it, what it offers and when it should stay silent, then take it through approval to launch.

Problem
Many teams want the same customers' attention. Sending everything is noise, not personalization.
My role
Lead CX Specialist, hyper-personalization squad. I own my campaigns on the business side.
Why it was complex
Five stages with different owners, Risk and Finance approval, three languages, and bank-wide contact rules.
What I contributed
Segments, triggers, eligibility rules, rewards and message sequences; approvals; hand-off; results review.
Delivered
Seven campaigns I own are marked published in the squad tracker; one more awaits approval and a weather-triggered journey is in development.

01

Worked example: setting a spend threshold

A reward should change behaviour, not pay customers for what they already do.

Business questionWhich spend threshold makes a task-and-reward campaign add spending, at a reward cost Finance will approve?
HypothesisThe right threshold sits a little above what most target customers spend today: close enough to reach, high enough to need a stretch.
EvidenceEach customer's recent monthly card spend. The figures below are synthetic.
Decision logicAt each threshold, size three groups: already qualify (cost without change), within reach (the real target) and out of reach (no effect). Move the threshold to see the trade-off.
Synthetic demonstration · 10,000 invented customers

Illustrates the method. Not bank data and not a campaign result. The verdict rules are illustrative.

500 AZN
already qualify: rewarded for spending they would do anyway
within reach: spend 60–100% of the threshold today (60% is an illustrative cut-off)
out of reach: spend under 60% of it, unlikely to change behaviour

ValidationA control group receives no message, so the campaign's effect is the difference between the groups, not the total spend.
Measurement planAdded spend per customer against control; reward cost per added AZN; participation; opt-outs and complaints as a guardrail.
What I decideFor the campaigns I own: the segment, the spend threshold within the period, the reward and the message sequence. Risk and Finance approve the cost.
02

Decision rules, in order

Every customer passes the same checks before a message goes out. No single team owns them all.

  1. Bank-wide filtersDo-not-contact lists, recently contacted customers
    Data Science engine
  2. EligibilityProduct held, spend threshold, segment
    My campaign rules
  3. Participation limitNo repeat reward for the same customer
    My campaign rules
  4. Trigger momentSalary arrival, a spending pattern, a date
    My campaign rules
  5. Launch sequencingNo mixed messages to one segment on the same day
    Squad governance
  6. Control groupHeld out so the effect can be measured
    Measurement design

Supporting example: a weather-triggered journey (in development)

Most triggers come from a customer's own transactions. I designed a scenario where a weather forecast chooses the day a seasonal dining offer runs, messages are sequenced ahead of that day, and participation is capped. The scenario design is done; the weather connection and platform build are with the engagement team, and launch is not confirmed. There are no results.

Constraints I worked within
  • Several approval points for each campaign, including Risk and Finance, with every customer-facing text in Azerbaijani, English and Russian before build.
  • Bank-wide filters and control groups applied by the Data Science scoring engine.
  • In May 2026 a content-system migration blocked several launches, so plans had to be resequenced.
